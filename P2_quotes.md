## P2-001 / P2-003 — Enders group (1949 work; 1954 Nobel lecture)

Source: Nobel Lecture PDF (official), file saved under session downloads; text in `wrk/p2_history/_notes/enders_nobel_lecture.txt`.

**Medium / enrichment**

> "fragments of chick amniotic membrane nourished with a balanced salt solution and ox serum ultrafiltrate"

> "the tissues were preserved while the nutritive medium was removed and replenished at frequent intervals. In this way viability of the cells could be maintained for 30 days or longer and full opportunity allowed for viral growth to occur"

> "Most widely used has been the synthetic-tissue culture medium, known as 199, developed by Morgan, Morton and Parker."

**Endpoint**

> "cytopathogenic effects of the virus, as we later called them"

> "the addition of type-specific antiserum to the suspended cell culture before the virus was introduced protected the cells from the destructive effect of the latter"

> "comparing at intervals the pH of the medium in inoculated cultures with [controls]"

**Note:** 1949 *Science* full PDF not open in this pass; medium description for Lansing work is carried by the Nobel lecture’s retrospective methods narrative + consistent secondary summaries.

## P2-009 — Fenner et al., Cultivation and Assay of Viruses (Medical Virology chapter, PMC7173454)

**Growth then maintenance (little or no serum)**

> "Fetal calf serum (5–10%) is therefore incorporated in the media used for the initial growth of cells in culture. Once the monolayer is established, the “growth medium” is removed, virus is inoculated, and “maintenance medium” containing little or no serum is added to the culture."

**Historical CPE inflection**

> "Since 1949, when Enders, Weller, and Robbins reported that poliovirus could be grown in cultured nonneural cells with the production of recognizable cytopathic changes, hundreds of previously unknown viruses have been isolated and identified in cell cultures."

## P2-010 — CLSI M41-A (2006) — from project institutional notes

> "Once the cultures have reached the required density, maintenance medium containing 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS) can be used in place of growth medium, which usually contains 10% FBS. Medium with the lower concentration of FBS will maintain cell viability and will slow monolayer overgrowth."

> "Typically 10% FBS is used for growth medium intended for the propagation of cell culture monolayers, while a concentration of 1 to 3% is utilized to maintain subconfluent to confluent monolayers both before and after inoculation."

> "Serum may also contain specific and nonspecific viral inhibitors and their presence may vary by lot. The lower serum concentration supports cell viability while permitting virus replication."

### P2-015 — Moffat diagnostic procedures (full PDF)

> "Growth medium usually consists of a synthetic medium such as 199 or Eagle's Basal Medium with added 10% calf serum. The serum may be varied in type or amount according to the virus or cells being cultured. After about 3 days the cultures are changed to maintenance medium which may contain 1% serum or be serum-free. Antibiotics are usually included."

### P2-017 — Subramanyam et al. 1961 vaccinia (full PDF)

> Growth: "HBSS, 80%; sheep serum, 20%; lactalbumin hydrolysate, 0.5%; yeast extract, 0.1%"

> "After good monolayer formation, usually 6 to 9 days after the preparation of the cultures, the medium was replaced with a maintenance medium consisting of EBSS with 10% sheep serum, the concentration of other substances being the same as in the growth medium. This medium was used for virus inoculation."

> "examined for cytopathic effects every 24 hours"

### P2-200 — Smith SE 1961 *A Maintenance Medium for Tissue Culture Virus Studies* (user ID)

> "The use of tissue culture techniques for studying viruses has led to a special requirement for a type of medium usually referred to as maintenance medium."

> "The standard media used for growing the cultures" do not necessarily satisfy virus-study needs.

> "The liver digest is used at a 10 per cent dilution in Earle's balanced salt solution to form the basic maintenance medium. In some systems a level of 5 per cent has proved adequate."

> "For titrations of poliovirus the cultures are kept on a serum free medium for periods of up to 12 days from infection. For safety testing of poliomyelitis vaccine, cultures are maintained for periods of up to 28 days… again on serum free medium."

> Lactalbumin hydrolysate medium "with added serum" is what cells "are usually grown" on; liver digest maintenance compared favorably to that and to S.M. 199.

> Cites Melnick and Riordan 1952 on lactalbumin hydrolysate in growth media.

### P2-201 — Hsiung GD 1961 *Applications of Primary Cell Cultures…* (user ID)

> "Takemoto and Habel found that 2 percent horse serum from certain horses when added to the overlay caused inhibition of 90 percent of the plaques produced by poliovirus type 1."

> Protein-free synthetic overlay medium discussed; lactalbumin hydrolysate in overlay can accelerate some ECHO plaques.

> "certain ECHO viruses produced plaques under agar overlay but were incapable of producing cytopathic changes in parallel fluid cultures."

## Round 8 — P2-008 full Chapter 3 (Schmidt 1969) screenshot PDF

**Source:** Lennette & Schmidt, *Diagnostic Procedures for Viral and Rickettsial Infections*, 4th ed., **Chapter 3** (not ch. 2), Nathalie J. Schmidt, book pp. ~79–178.  
**File:** `wrk/p2_history/P2-008 - Diagnostic Procedures... chapter 3 tissue culture technics.pdf`  
**Read method:** page images (no embedded text); readable.

### Growth vs maintenance (pp. 90–91)

> "The nutrient media employed for cell culture in virology may be divided into two broad categories, **growth media and maintenance media**."

> "**Growth media** are employed for initiation of the cultures and usually contain serum in combination with other substances for promoting **rapid cellular proliferation**. **Prior to inoculation of the cultures with virus, the growth medium is removed** and the cultures are washed with a balanced salt solution to remove antibodies or other viral inhibitory substances which may be present in the serum used for growth (4, 149). Fetal or agamma calf serum is usually free from viral inhibitory substances, and if these are used for outgrowth it is not necessary to wash the cultures prior to using them for viral propagation."

> "**Maintenance media** are intended to keep the cultures in a **slow, steady state of metabolism** during the time of viral proliferation, and they are **less rich** than growth media in substances promoting rapid cellular metabolism and proliferation. **Maintenance media for viral propagation should be serum-free if possible, but for certain cell types serum is required.** In these instances fetal or agamma calf serum is generally most suitable."

### Explicit recipes (selected; volumes ≈ per 100 ml)

**Human amnion — growth (p. 103)**  
Fetal bovine serum **20.0 ml** + Medium #199 **76.5 ml** → **~20% FBS**  
**Maintenance for viral propagation (p. 103):** FBS **2.0 ml** + Medium #199 **94.5 ml** → **2% FBS**

**Human fetal kidney — growth (p. 103)**  
FBS **20.0 ml** + Eagle’s MEM **76.4 ml** → **~20% FBS**  
**Maintenance (p. 104):** FBS **2.0 ml** + Eagle’s MEM **94.5 ml** → **2% FBS** (or L-15 + 2 ml FBS)

**Human fetal skin/muscle & diploid strains — growth (pp. 104–105)**  
FBS **10.0 ml** + Eagle’s MEM **86.5 ml** → **10% FBS**  
**Maintenance for virus (p. 105):** FBS **2.0 ml** + Eagle’s MEM **94.5 ml** or L-15 **97.0 ml** → **2% FBS**

**Continuous passage lines — maintenance (p. 107)**  
i. Chicken or fetal bovine serum **5.0 ml** + Medium #199 **42.0 ml** + lactalbumin hydrolysate-yeast extract **50.0 ml** → **~5% serum**  
ii. FBS **5.0 ml** + Leibovitz L-15 **94.0 ml** → **5% FBS**

**Monkey kidney — growth (p. 109)**  
Bovine serum **5.0 ml** + 5% lactalbumin hydrolysate **10.0 ml** + Hanks’ BSS **82.0 ml** → **5% serum**  
**Maintenance for viral propagation (p. 109):** serum-free Eagle’s MEM (Earle’s BSS) with antibiotics; or options with **2% FBS** (p. 110–111 for BS-C-1 / related)

**BS-C-1 / rabbit kidney — maintenance (pp. 111–112)**  
FBS **2.0 ml** + Eagle’s MEM **~94–95 ml** → **2% FBS**  
Also serum-free Eagle MEM or L-15 options for monkey kidney viral propagation (p. 110).

**Respiratory viruses (p. 109)**  
> "The most satisfactory maintenance medium for isolation and propagation of certain respiratory viruses, which are inhibited by high concentrations of NaHCO₃, is **Leibovitz Medium No. 15 containing 2% fetal bovine serum** and 1% of the penicillin-streptomycin-neomycin-bacitracin solution."

**Isolation / CPE / controls (pp. 114–116)**

> Cultures "observed microscopically for evidence of a viral CPE"

> "Certain inocula may be toxic to the cells and cause their degeneration before specific viral cytopathic activity can be manifested… cultures showing marked degeneration within the first 24 to 48 hr… should be subpassaged… Also, cultures may undergo spontaneous degeneration… indicated by degeneration of **uninoculated control tubes** incubated with the test series"

## Round 9 — New PDFs (006, 034, 042, 043, 205, 206)

### P2-006 — Melnick 1955 *Ann NY Acad Sci*

> "inexpensive medium which is simple to prepare. It consists of **0.5 per cent lactalbumin enzymatic hydrolysate, 2 per cent calf serum, and 97.5 per cent Hank's salt solution**"

> After inoculum: "replaced with 1 ml of fresh lactalbumin hydrolysate medium in Earle's solution. The cultures are observed on the 2nd, 4th, 7th and 10th days for **cytopathic changes**"

Cites: Diagnostic Procedures 2nd ed; Melnick & Riordan 1952; Enders group; Weller et al. 1952.

### P2-034 — Morgan, Morton, Parker 1950 Medium 199

> "complexity and variability of these natural substrates [plasma, serum, exudates, extracts] has made it almost impossible to employ them in experiments designed to determine the substances that are actually utilized by the cells"

> Attempt "synthetic medium... in the absence of blood serum and embryo extract. Although a completely adequate synthetic mixture has not yet been achieved..."

> Often used "preliminary feeding mixture containing naturally-occurring ingredients (**horse serum and embryo extract**)"

### P2-042 — Li & Schaeffer 1953 *Science*

> "**Horse serum is frequently inhibitory to virus**, and chick embryo extracts are at times sources of contamination."

> "**Medium E**... **75% Hank-Simms**... and **25% of the bovine plasma hydrolyzate**... Simms serum ultrafiltrate may be omitted entirely with equally good results."

> "nonspecific degeneration does not readily occur in uninfected tissue, [so] the specific effects may be more easily [read]"

### P2-043 — Melnick & Opton WHO assay (PMC)

> "simple **lactalbumin hydrolysate medium** is used" for outgrowth and colour test

> "The **nutrient medium**, used for cell suspensions and cell titrations, is prepared by adding **2.0% calf serum**"

> "substitution of the simple lactalbumin hydrolysate medium for the more complex media like **Connaught No. 199**"

### P2-205 — Schmidt 1972 *Am J Clin Pathol*

### P2-206 — Melnick 1953 *Proc Soc Exp Biol Med* (dry-tube culture)

> "**Complete medium**... **9 parts of mixture No. 199 and 1 part bovine serum**" [= **10% serum**]

> "complete medium was removed, the tubes were **washed with**... **Earle's salt solution (to remove poliomyelitis virus inhibitory substances present in bovine serum)**, and... **maintenance medium consisting of 1 part of No. 199 and 1 part of Earle's salt solution**" [= **serum-free maintenance**]

> "concentrations of **10%** when used with No. 199 were satisfactory for good outgrowth" (Fig. 3 serum concentration study)
